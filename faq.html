About me

It can be tricky for non-Mandarin speakers, so here is a quick guide to my name, Qingguang Zheng (郑庆广).

My family name, Zheng, sounds like JUNG. My given name, Qingguang, has two syllables: CHING + GWAHNG.

“Qing” rhymes with “sing” or “king”. “Guang” is “gwa” plus the “-ng” in “song”.

All together: CHING-GWAHNG JUNG. I’m also happy to go by Victor.

Victoir is my online handle, used for this site and my GitHub. In person, Victor works fine.

It’s a Q and a Z, my initials: the Z sits inside the Q, and its base sweeps out to become the Q’s tail.

Research

I study what happens when people talk: the topics they raise, the words they use (for example “we” versus “I”), and how their language, facial expressions, gaze and even brain activity fall into or out of sync. So far I’ve mostly studied couples, asking how these patterns relate to relationship quality and well-being. At UConn I’m approaching the same questions through ecological psychology and language & cognition, treating conversation as a coordinated activity between people.

Coding hours of conversation by hand is slow and hard to keep consistent. With carefully designed prompts, language models can get close to trained human coders, which makes it practical to study much richer interaction data. Humans still need to check the results.
