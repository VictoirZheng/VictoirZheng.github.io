Hi, I’m Victor.

Qingguang Zheng 郑庆广

I’m a Ph.D. student in Psychological Sciences at the University of Connecticut, in the Ecological Psychology and Language & Cognition tracks.

I’m fascinated by what happens between people when they talk: how two people’s words, faces and rhythms start to move together, and why some conversations leave us feeling understood while others don’t. So far, most of my work has looked at couples and families.

Away from the desk, you’ll probably find me over a Go board, watching Chelsea, or deep in a video game.

Things I’m curious about

When do two people fall into sync?

Conversation is something we do together. I look at how partners’ language, facial expressions, gaze and even brain activity coordinate over time, and what that coordination says about a relationship.

What do our words give away?

Small choices, like saying “we” instead of “I”, can carry a lot. I use language analysis to study how couples talk, and how that relates to how close they feel.

Can machines help us see interaction better?

Coding hours of conversation by hand is slow. I’ve been exploring how language models and computer vision can work as careful research assistants, with humans in the loop.
