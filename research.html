Research

My research asks how people coordinate in conversation, and what that coordination means for their relationships and well-being. At UConn I’m approaching these questions through ecological psychology and the study of language and cognition.

Below is the work I did at Beijing Normal University with Prof. Xiaoyi Fang and Prof. Yanchao Bi.

Past projects

Couples in conversation

Beijing Normal University · 2020–2026 · two NSFC-funded studies

Longitudinal studies of newlywed and married couples that combine recorded conversations, fNIRS hyperscanning and questionnaires. I worked on turning hours of video into data (transcripts, topics, facial expressions, gaze) and on measuring how partners synchronize. Along the way I built an LLM-assisted pipeline for coding what couples talk about.

Dyadic InteractionSynchronyfNIRSNLPLLMsOpenFace

Student mental health, at national scale

Beijing Normal University · 2022–2026 · Ministry of Education

Yearly analyses of mental health among Chinese college students, and an ongoing revision of a national screening scale. Mostly, this meant building reproducible R and Python pipelines that could handle millions of responses.

PsychometricsLarge-Scale DataReproducibility

The shape of emotion words

Beijing Normal University · 2020–2022 · undergraduate research

How each of us organizes emotion words in our heads, and whether that personal “semantic space” relates to mood, social values and moral judgment.

Semantic SpaceRSAMachine Learning

Day to day I work in Python and R, with Mplus for structural equation models.
